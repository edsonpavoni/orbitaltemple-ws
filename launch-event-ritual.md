## Political Speech Draft (~1 min)

---

You are not accepted.

We all have been there. The first broken heart. The job you didn't get. The group of friends you wished were yours.

But from all the denials of acceptance we experience in life, the most consequential, the most under the radar, is the acceptance to heaven. To paradise. To transcendence after death.

If you were born Black in 1452, you could be enslaved because a Pope authorized it for "salvation of souls."

If you were born Indigenous in Canada between 1869 and 1996, you could be torn from your family, buried in unmarked graves, because churches needed to "kill the Indian, save the man."

If you were born gay in Saudi Arabia today, you could face beheading because your love threatens the community's path to paradise.

I myself, born in Brazil, in the outskirts of São Paulo — Christianity offered me a path away from the streets. But that same faith told me that loving the wrong person meant hell.

The faith that gave me community also made me participate in a system that oppresses the ones who are not accepted.

This is not ancient history. This is now.

But what if the imaginary of our afterlife was actually inclusive?

Tonight, we open those doors.

Tonight, anyone's name can ascend.
