# From the Artist

Heaven is not a place you go when you die.
Heaven is a construction of acceptance.
It determines who belongs here, now, in this life.

In the 1500s, heaven meant certain things.
Certain people.

In 1654, the idea of heaven was used to justify
enslaving Black people from Africa.

Today, women are denied education in the name of heaven.
LGBTQ+ people are killed because we say they don't deserve it.
Abortion is restricted because of who we believe belongs there.

The pattern is clear.
The pattern is brutal.

Those we ban from heaven
suffer more discrimination,
make less money,
live shorter lives.

On a global scale, we literally kill people
that we feel do not deserve heaven.


Orbital Temple is a place in heaven
for all the people that have no place there.

For the ones that don't fit.


Heaven is colonized.

The decolonization of heaven
is freedom to the minds and bodies
of billions of people.


Faith is an important part of being human.
It is a powerful tool for belonging,
for transcending,
for love.

But it is also being used
as a powerful force of discrimination
and prejudice.

We can hold both truths.
We must hold both truths.


Orbital Temple doesn't ask you to abandon faith.
It asks: who decides?

Who decides who belongs in heaven?

What if that decision belonged to all of us?
What if heaven was not a gate to pass through
but a place we build together?


You belong here.
You always belonged here.
Welcome home.
